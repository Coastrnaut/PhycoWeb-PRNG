> This feature makes no security claim. No PhycoWeb-PRNG model, `phyco-v3` included, may be used
> to generate secrets; use an established cryptographic random-number generator for keys,
> nonces, tokens and salts. Passing statistical tests does not show cryptographic security.

## Context

`phyco-v2` (feature 002; `design.md` 1.0, SHA-256 `a1109bf1…`; `phycoweb/model_v2.py`, SHA-256
`1260142d…`) has passed every statistical attempt it was given: SP 800-22 attempt 1 in feature
002 and battery attempt 1 (PractRand and TestU01) in feature 003. It can pass a test and still
not keep a secret. security.md §9.2 records why it cannot support any cryptographic claim:

- **S1–S3**: its seed is 64 bits, so no security level can exceed 64 bits; trying every seed
  costs about 2⁷² generator steps.
- **S13**: its step is not one-to-one. States merge through the vein decay and through a zero
  mode of the transport.
- **S12, S14**: every vein stays in 0…2³¹, and the top bits of each vein are strongly biased.
- **S15**: that bound holds from every starting state, so a 2×2 ring that keeps the design-1.0
  vein update reaches at most about 2²⁵² secret states, short of the 2²⁵⁶ that FUT-SEC-2 requires
  for a 128-bit claim.
- **S11**: its statistical quality came from two added ARX terms, not from a designed component.

This feature starts the route the strategy note recommends (option A2,
[`crypto-redesign-strategy.md`](../../research/security/crypto-redesign-strategy.md) §4): a new
keyless, bio-inspired permutation Π on a parameterised grid, used in a mode whose published
security proof reduces the generator to Π. The default mode is a keyed duplex. The feature ends
with a frozen design, `phyco-v3`, or with a recorded decision to stop (option C, security.md
§21).

**This feature makes no security claim.** Under the constitution's Security Policy, no
cryptographic claim may be made, and no evaluation meant to support one pursued, until FUT-SEC-1
to FUT-SEC-9 are met. Design work and the search for weaknesses are allowed, and their results
must be reported (security.md §15). Everything this spec asks of the design (the security
target, its parameters, its bounds, the mode) is a requirement the design must meet, not a
statement that it does. In this feature `phyco-v3` can reach claim level L1 (security target
specified); L2 is started but not reached, because the internal attack campaign and the
reduced-round statistical margin belong to feature 005.

**This feature changes no existing generator.** `phyco-v2`, `lut-v1`, harness `sp80022` 1.0.0,
the feature-002 and feature-003 protocols, their seed sets and every frozen file stay as they
are. `phyco-v2` is copied byte for byte into `baseline/` (Principle V). The live files are not
moved, because the freeze records of features 002 and 003 hash them.

**External review.** An external design review before the freeze is in scope if one can be
arranged (security.md §7). The independent review of claim level L5 belongs to feature 009 and
is out of scope. The review asks for comments on a design that makes no claim. It is not an
evaluation intended to support a claim, which the Security Policy defers, and is never cited as
one; the review package (FR-063) carries the disclaimer and says so.

**The rules that select the design are fixed in this spec before any design result exists**
(section "Pre-registered Design Protocol"). After approval they may only become stricter
(Principle IV).

**Inputs.** The four documents this spec answers to are the strategy note
([`crypto-redesign-strategy.md`](../../research/security/crypto-redesign-strategy.md)),
[`security.md`](../../security.md) 2.9, the seed-entropy note
([`seed-entropy-bound.md`](../../research/security/seed-entropy-bound.md)) and the hardware
note ([`vhdl-genai.md`](../../research/hardware/vhdl-genai.md)). The hardware note's rules for
AI-assisted RTL bind features 006 and 008, but three of them reach back into this feature: the
Python model is the only oracle, the oracle and its corner-case vectors exist before any RTL,
and the design document leaves an implementer, human or model, nothing to guess.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

Users: (a) the repository owner, who decides whether `phyco-v3` goes on to cryptanalysis
(feature 005) or the route stops; (b) reviewers, the owner now and an external cryptographer if
one can be arranged, who need a complete design rationale and a review package; (c) the later
implementers of the C stream and the VHDL (features 006 and 008), people or AI drafting tools
whose drafts deterministic tools judge, who need a design with nothing left to guess and an
oracle to check against; (d) researchers, who must be able to reproduce every figure.

### User Story 1 - The 64-bit seed bound of phyco-v2, reproduced (Priority: P1)

The owner sees `phyco-v2`'s 64-bit bound demonstrated as an executed attack rather than a
counting argument. With two of the four seed words given to the attacker, all 2³² candidates
for the other two are tried, the seed is recovered from three output words, and the cost per
candidate is measured and scaled to the full 2⁶⁴ seeds. This happens before any key interface
for `phyco-v3` is implemented, and it is the first validation of the analysis tooling on a
known result.

**Why this priority**: FUT-SEC-8 puts it first. It needs no new design, it turns S3 from a
generic argument into a reproduced result, and it validates the search tool before any negative
result from the tooling is allowed to count.

**Independent Test**: Run the pre-registered demonstration on its development targets. Check
that each recovered seed equals its target, that the false matches agree with the random
baseline, and that the cost is recorded with its commands and hashes. Agreement is tested on the
one-word false-match count of each target, against a Poisson distribution of mean
(2³² − 1)/2³², and on the three targets pooled, against three times that mean, two-sided at level
0.01; any false match after two or three words is reported as a deviation. A disagreement is a
finding (FR-060), analysed as the protocol says, and never a reason to sweep again.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** `phyco-v2` (`phycoweb/model_v2.py`, SHA-256 `1260142d…`) and a development target
   seed whose first two words are revealed, **When** every one of the 2³² candidates for the
   last two words is tested against the first three output words, **Then** the target is among
   the matches and every match is listed.
2. **Given** the sweep, **When** its record is read, **Then** it gives the matches after one, two
   and three words beside their expected numbers, the cost per candidate in generator steps and
   in time, the total cost, and the extrapolation to all 2⁶⁴ seeds.
3. **Given** the search tool, **When** it is checked before the sweep, **Then** its output
   matches the `phyco-v2` golden vectors word for word.
4. **Given** the demonstration report, **When** the owner has reviewed it, **Then** the review is
   recorded before any `phyco-v3` instantiation (key-loading) code exists.

### User Story 3 - The permutation, its inverse and the mode, specified and modelled (Priority: P3)

### Edge Cases

- **The demonstration misses its target**: the search tool is defective and fails its
  validation. The defect is reported and fixed, and the sweep is repeated under
  `--repeat-after-defect`, on the owner's request and charged to research R7's feature-wide row
  (Pre-registered Design Protocol, "FUT-SEC-8 demonstration"), its record written beside the
  first, which stays, and naming the defect, the record it supersedes and the
  owner's request with its date (data-model §4); every run is reported. The
  demonstration uses development seeds and decides nothing about a design, so a
  repeat is not re-seeding until passing.
- **More matches than expected**: any extra match after three words is listed and separated with
  a fourth word, and the count is reported against the baseline.
- **No published theorem covers the mode exactly, or a precondition cannot be met**: the mode is
  changed to one that a theorem covers, or the feature stops. A mode without a matching theorem
  is never adopted.
- **A size fails a generic bound**: it becomes a research variant, outside every claim, and the
  production interface refuses it.
- **Odd grid sizes**: a red-black update needs an even torus. An odd size uses a three-colour
  schedule from the same generics, or is listed as unsupported with the reason, as an open item
  against FUT-GRID-1. The schedule is a function of n's parity, computed at elaboration from one
  stated rule (two colours for even n, three for odd n), so an odd n is the same core with
  another generic value (FR-018, FR-019). The rule must give a proper colouring of the torus,
  wrap-around links included, at every odd n from 3 to 15, which a committed test checks link by
  link; (i + j) mod 3 does not, at n = 7 and 13 (FR-018).
- **A candidate fails an inverse or bijectivity test**: the failure is investigated first. A
  recorded tool defect gets the one re-run the protocol allows, inside the budget; otherwise the
  candidate is rejected and logged with the failure, never deleted, and it counts towards the
  stop rule's budget. An operation list not made of the two allowed step forms (research R11) is
  refused before any test.
- **A candidate is to be dropped for a reason the rules do not name**: it cannot be. `rejected`
  and `ineligible` are recorded only for the rules' reasons: a failed inverse or bijectivity
  test, a failed eligibility item or a spent budget. A candidate may be withdrawn before its
  first tool run for any recorded reason, and it still counts towards the stop rule; the log
  writer refuses any other decision (FR-041).
- **A run is suspected of a tool defect**: a tool defect is one of a failed validation or
  re-validation; a disagreement between avalanche counts and dependency matrices; a
  disagreement between two evaluators of one operation list (scalar, batch, C, model); a crash or an
  undocumented exit status; a reproduction that differs from its original on a field that enters a
  verdict, in the environment that made the original (FR-053); or a bug in
  the tool's code, shown by the faulty line and by a test that fails before
  the fix and passes after. It is recorded with that evidence before any
  re-run. A disliked result is none of these.
- **A tool fails its validation**: none of its results count, no bound from it enters the round
  rule, and the failure is recorded. The defect is recorded with its cause and fixed, and the
  tool is validated again on its second known result, fixed before its first
  validation, as well as the first, so that a fix cannot be tuned to one
  value (a tool with none stays unvalidated, its results `not evaluated`;
  FR-039); attempts are not limited, but each is recorded. Results of the failed
  version never count; results already counted from an earlier validated version stand, since
  they cite its hash. A trail or distinguisher found by a tool not yet validated is never
  dropped: it is recorded as an unverified hypothesis (FR-060) and blocks the candidate's
  eligibility until a validated tool, or a direct evaluation of the trail with the validated
  exact models, settles it (FR-039).
- **A validation target's published value cannot be obtained**: the tool stays unvalidated and
  its results are `not evaluated`; the owner may provide the value with its source (research
  R19). If no trail route is validated, no candidate can establish R_bound, none is eligible,
  and the feature recommends stopping (stop rule).
- **A paper that a mode theorem rests on cannot be obtained or read**: its theorem stays
  `unverified`, and `downloads.json` records the file as `not obtained` with the reason (research
  R19). Where another theorem of research R10's list covers the same call and its paper is on disk
  and read, the mode chapter uses it, in a new design-document version reviewed again; where none
  does, no published theorem's preconditions can be shown met, and the feature recommends stopping
  (stop rule). Another copy of the paper, from any source, is a new download that needs the owner's
  permission for that file (FR-046; research R19).
- **No proven or validated trail bound reaches 2⁻¹²⁸ at a claimable size**: R_bound is not
  established for that candidate within its analysis budget, and it cannot be frozen. The gap is
  reported, and the candidate is `ineligible`; another candidate is entered in its place, before the
  set closes, while its family has fewer than three evaluated candidates and the budget allows (each
  of the replacement's rows holding more than zero hours; stop rule), as the candidate log's
  `replacement` entry records (section "Pre-registered Design Protocol",
  "Eligibility and selection"); if no candidate ends eligible, the feature recommends stopping.
- **The round rule gives an expensive R**: rounds are never lowered for throughput. The cost is
  reported, and an expensive R is never a reason to enter or select another candidate: a further
  candidate is entered only in place of one `rejected` or `ineligible`, within the stop rule's
  budget, and selection follows the rules (section "Pre-registered Design Protocol", "Eligibility
  and selection").
- **A distinguisher on more rounds turns up before the freeze**: R rises by the round rule, and
  every measurement the raised R needs runs as a new measurement of its kind in the same budget row
  (research R7). If a row cannot hold it, that evidence is inconclusive and the candidate
  `ineligible` at that size for a spent budget, and selection proceeds among the others within the
  stop rule; if a selection record exists, `round_rule.py --select` runs again over the whole set
  and writes the next one, and a newly selected candidate gets the security-target review and every
  later review, each in a row of its own (section "Pre-registered Design Protocol", "Eligibility and
  selection"). A raised R is never by itself a reason to drop a candidate. A finding that breaks the
  target on the full rounds is recorded at once
  (FR-051). If it comes after the owner design review, the design document gets a new version,
  the rationale is revised, the review is repeated for the changed parts and recorded in a row of
  its own, "Owner design review <k>" (the notes under the Review & Approval table), and the review
  package is rebuilt (FR-043).
- **A change is needed after the freeze** (to a frozen file, a raise of R by feature 005, a new
  claimable size): it is a new model ID with its own vectors and freeze (FR-028), and the frozen
  files are never edited. Before the freeze, a change is a new design-document version and a
  log entry.
- **The frozen design document and the frozen model disagree**: the frozen model and vectors are
  the expected values (FR-056), and the design document is the specification. A disagreement
  found after the freeze is a finding; the frozen files stay unchanged, and the correction is a
  new model ID with its own design-document version, model, vectors and freeze (FR-028), even
  where the intended design is the same. If it bears on any claim, it is recorded at once
  (FR-051).
- **Both nonlinearity families score the same**: the pre-registered tie-break decides.
- **No external reviewer can be arranged**: the freeze proceeds, and the review is recorded as
  not arranged (security.md §7).
- **A request exceeds an output limit, or production is asked for a reduced variant**: the
  interface refuses with an error. It never wraps, truncates or carries on silently.
- **Nonce reuse**: the same key, nonce and personalization string give the same output. This is
  stated behaviour (T7), not detected, within one object or across objects. No bounded record
  could cover the per-key instantiation limit, other objects or other devices, and the RTL keeps
  no nonce history, so a partial check would make the model differ from the hardware and
  suggest a protection that does not exist. A test shows the stated behaviour (FR-058).
- **Reseed without a validated entropy source**: the interface allows it, but it gives no
  prediction resistance, and its input is treated as chosen by the attacker (T9).
- **The Python model is slow at large grids**: vectors at large sizes may be shorter than at
  4×4, with the length and the measured speed stated.
- **Large evidence records** (for example dependency matrices at 16×16): the plan decides what is
  committed, compressed or regenerated on demand, and every record's hash is kept. An output kept
  only locally must come from a deterministic command, so that its committed SHA-256 can be
  checked by regenerating it, and its record states that command's cost (heavy or not, hours
  projected). An output that is not deterministic, such as a solver log with timings, is never
  local-only: it is committed, compressed, or its verdict rests on a committed deterministic
  summary. Local-only outputs are written under `data/local/004/<record_id>/`, which `.gitignore`
  ignores, never under the reports directory, and a committed output above 10 MB is refused
  (research R16).
- **An edge state that no key reaches**: it still gets a constructed-state vector, and the record
  says that no key reaches it, as no seed reaches class 3 in `phyco-v2` (vhdl-genai.md §5.2).
- **The second transcription disagrees with the model**: the disagreement is a finding. Its cause
  is found and recorded, and the design document, the model or the transcription is corrected
  before the freeze; a correction to the design document is a new version of it.
- **Evidence is missing for a result**: the status is "not evaluated", "inconclusive" or
  "blocked" (security.md §16.3). A generated report is not proof that a test ran.
- **The bias rule fires on its uniform control**: the bias evidence of that size is inconclusive for
  every candidate, since the control's one record per test and size serves them all, so none is
  eligible at that size and the feature recommends stopping (stop rule), citing the control record.
  The control inputs are never redrawn or replaced, and the rule is not loosened (Principle IV).
- **Property-based testing is not approved**: the boundary and fixed tests stand alone, and the
  gap is recorded as an open item (FR-058).
- **An AI session offers a review**: it is recorded as an AI review, never as the external design

**Security target (L1; FUT-SEC-1 to FUT-SEC-6, FUT-SEC-9)**

- **FR-004**: The security target MUST state each property sought as a bound on an adversary's
  advantage, in the definitions of security.md §3: keystream security for nonces never repeated
  under a key, multi-user security for u keys, and backtracking resistance across each forget
  call. Prediction resistance MUST be stated as not sought (D7; FUT-SEC-4). Every bound MUST be
  stated relative to Π as a random permutation and limited to attacks below the target
  strength, as in a flat sponge claim (strategy note §2.1), and the one assumption it rests on,
  that Π has no structural distinguisher below 2¹²⁸, MUST be named as what the cryptanalysis
  attacks. Backtracking resistance means that a state compromised between calls reveals nothing
  about output delivered before the last forget call, and that a state compromised within a
  request reveals, through Π⁻¹, only that request's earlier blocks back to its last forget call,
  at most the per-request cap of 2¹⁹ bits. Instantiate ends with a forget call (FR-013), so no
  such walk reaches the key. Every cost in the security target, in FR-005 and in the round
  rule's R_dist is counted in one unit, evaluations of Π or Π⁻¹ (research R9's N), with every
  data block observed counted as at least one evaluation, and an attack's data is also bounded
  by the output limits (FR-007).
- **FR-005**: The target strength MUST be s = 128 bits at every claimable size, and no parameter

  model. The generator cannot check this, a key with less entropy caps the strength at that
  entropy (FR-010), and the security target lists the assumption among the caller's
  obligations. The key is the entropy input at instantiation, so its entropy exceeds the target
  strength, as SP 800-90A requires of entropy input (seed-entropy note §3). The nonce MUST be
  128 bits, and a nonce MUST be used at most once per key (FUT-SEC-3), in one of two ways: a
  counter per key, never repeated or wrapped, kept by the caller across objects and devices; or

  a term of the mode's bound, with a per-key limit of at most 2³² instantiations so that the
  term stays within ε (FR-007). The generator cannot tell a random nonce from a counter value, so
  this limit applies to every key whatever its nonces: one instantiation counter per object, and
  across objects a usage rule (research R9). An optional personalization string MUST have a stated
  maximum length and MUST NOT replace the nonce. The behaviour on a repeated nonce MUST be stated

- **FR-007**: The security target MUST specify the maximum output per instantiation (key and

- **FR-008**: The threat model MUST adopt each of T1–T11 (security.md §5) for `phyco-v3` or

**Mode (D1)**

- **FR-013**: The generator MUST be a keyed duplex over Π with the calls instantiate (key, nonce,
  personalization string), generate (n bits), forget and an optional reseed (input), and forget
  MUST follow every generate request and end every instantiate, in the form the theorem covers
  (research R10), so that no state compromise reaches the key (FR-004). Its security bound MUST
  come from a named, published theorem for exactly this mode (for example the keyed-duplex
  bounds of Daemen, Mennink and Van Assche, 2017, or the sponge-based generator of Bertoni et
  al., 2010). Every precondition of the theorem, including how the key enters the state, MUST be
  listed with the design feature that meets it. The key and the nonce are placed in the initial
  state at stated positions, the rest of it set to stated constants, as the full-state keyed
  duplex initializes; later inputs (the personalization string and reseed inputs) are absorbed
  over the rate only (FR-018), and the mode chapter MUST show that this is a special case of the
  theorem's absorption. Both are preconditions, "unverified" until the paper is read; if the
  paper's form differs, the design takes the paper's form, or the feature stops. The reachable
  secret state MUST stay at least 2s at every call boundary (FR-009), and a reseed never resets
  a counter. In the model the key is given once, when the generator object is constructed, and
  held until zeroization: each instantiate takes the nonce and the personalization string and
  loads the stored key into the state, so no key is ever compared with another. In hardware the key
  is loaded once, along a registered shift chain, into key registers at the nodes whose lanes hold
  key bits, and zeroized with the state; at every instantiate the host gives the nonce on the
  absorption interface, and in one cycle a selector at every lane sets the lane from one of four
  sources: a key register, the nonce chain, the nonce register of a rate lane that holds nonce bits,
  or its stated constant (FR-025 "Key loading and reset").
- **FR-014**: Padding and domain separation MUST be specified so that every absorbed input (key,
  nonce, personalization string, reseed input) and every call type can be decoded uniquely from
  the absorbed blocks and is separated from every other, and this injectivity MUST be shown in
  two ways: a proof in the design document that the encoding of every (call type, input) pair
  into absorbed blocks is injective, with the padding rule, the rule for partial blocks and the
  domain bits stated; and a committed check over every input length from 0 to each input's
  maximum and every call type, showing that distinct (call type, length) pairs give distinct
  block layouts, with a test that distinct development inputs give distinct block sequences.
  Acceptance is 0 collisions and the proof accepted at the owner design review. Where call
  types use distinct constant streams, the proof says whether the streams or the domain bits
  carry the separation. Padding constants and domain separators are constants of the mode, with
  their rule and reason stated (FR-034). In hardware a personalization string or a reseed input
  arrives after one 32-bit word that gives its length in bytes, so the core knows the length before
  it pads the input, and the unused bytes of a last partial word must be zero (FR-025 "Flow
  control").
- **FR-015**: The capacity MUST be c ≥ 256 bits at every claimable size, with rate r = b − c.
  Output MUST come only from the outer (rate) part, after a full call of Π. One canonical
  serialization of the output (word width, byte order, bit order) MUST be stated for every tool
  and for the hardware export. A request whose length is not a multiple of r keeps the first
  bits of its last block in that order and discards the rest (FR-027). Every digest of output,
  such as the long-run digest of the vectors, is taken over this serialization as raw bytes
  (4·2²⁰ bytes for 2²⁰ words of 32 bits), never over a text rendering of it.
- **FR-016**: The reseed input MUST be treated as chosen by the attacker (T9), and reseeding MUST
  NOT weaken any property sought. This feature has no validated entropy source and seeks no
  prediction resistance (D7), so no source is excepted; a later feature that brings a validated
  entropy source and seeks prediction resistance states its own rule under its own protocol. To make
  that verifiable, the mode chapter MUST map reseed to the theorem's absorption call with
  attacker-chosen input and list that mapping as a precondition, "unverified" until the paper
  is read (research R10); the reachable secret state after each reseed is at least 2s (FR-009);
  and a reseed never resets a counter. A reseed input's length is public even when its content
  is secret (FR-020).
- **FR-017**: The claimable sizes MUST be decided, which resolves the FUT-SEC-2 and FUT-GRID-2
  conflict at 2×2 (security.md §20.1). By default (D3) they start at 4×4, and 2×2 and 3×3 are
  reduced research variants outside every claim. The decision MUST be recorded in security.md
  §20.1 with the reason it weakens neither requirement: FUT-SEC-1 and FUT-SEC-2 bound a claimed
  strength, no claim covers a research size, each research row of the size profile states its
  key entropy c, its s = c/2 and the status `research`, and the production interface refuses
  every research variant (FR-033).

**Permutation Π (FUT-GRID-1 to FUT-GRID-5)**

- **FR-018**: Π MUST be a keyless permutation of the whole state of an n×n torus grid, with grid
  size n and word width w as generics covering every n from 2 to 16 (FUT-GRID-1). Each node
  holds two w-bit lanes (pressure and vein), so the state has b = 2w·n² bits (D3). Data MUST
  flow only between direct neighbours, with at most four channels per node: a channel is one
  w-bit word passed from a node to a neighbour in one phase, and a node receives at most four
  per phase (one from each neighbour, or two from each of two), counted from the operation list
  with the linear layer between boxes included. At 2×2 a node's opposite neighbours coincide, so its
  four links join two distinct nodes, two channels from each, and a translation by one node is its
  own inverse; the size profile states this, and 2×2 evidence counts only for properties that do not
  depend on the neighbourhood (FR-032, FR-052). Round constants MUST enter at local food nodes,
  placed by a stated rule (FUT-GRID-4) whose placement has no symmetry of the torus that moves a
  node at any claimable size: one food node is fixed by a rotation about it and two by the half-turn
  about their midpoint, so the rule takes at least three food nodes in an asymmetric arrangement,
  such as (0,0), (0,1) and (1,2), which has none at 4×4, 8×8 and 16×16 (computed on 2026-11-01), and
  research R13's symmetry check stays as written there. At 2×2 and 3×3, where every placement has
  such a symmetry (computed the same day), the placement is checked with the constants it carries as
  one pattern, the `symmetry` record says so, the size profile marks the variant, and no claim
  covers it (D3; research R13). Absorption and output MUST use registered trees over the
  rate lanes only, and the key and the nonce reach their lanes by the key path of FR-025 ("Key
  loading and reset"). The torus MUST be realisable as a folded torus, so that every link, the
  wrap-around links included, joins neighbouring nodes of the layout (Principle III; strategy
  note §5.4): logical neighbours sit at most two positions apart in each dimension of the
  layout, for odd n as well as even, with at most one node between a link's ends, and the

  MUST give a proper colouring of the torus, wrap-around links included, at every odd n from 3 to
  15, and a committed test checks every link at each odd n, since a rule as simple as (i + j) mod 3

  = (f(i) + f(j)) mod 3, with f a proper 3-colouring of the n-cycle such as f(k) = k mod 2 for k < n
  − 1 and f(n − 1) = 2, since two neighbours differ in one coordinate, where f differs by 1 or 2.

  claimable size, and at 2×2, and at a supported 3×3, at least one keyed reduced variant, such

  permutation-only. Per claimable size it gives the start-up self-test's expected output
  (FR-059). For every supported size the design document states the number of food nodes, their
  positions by the rule and the reason for them (for example the number of rounds until every
  lane depends on a constant injected in round 1, from the dependency propagation of research
  R13); the ROM depth at each food node (R entries per call type, times the number of distinct

- **FR-025**: The hardware structure of Π and the mode MUST meet these rules, each stated in the
  design document (feature-002 O1; FUT-HW-1, FUT-HW-5; security.md §13; checklist group E). For
  eligibility, decided for every candidate before selection, the stages, the chains per phase and
  the key path come from the candidate's operation-list declarations, checked by code; the interface

  - **Stages.** On any path, each pipeline stage of the round structure holds at most one serial
    w-bit carry chain from the round's updates, by the phase rule (FR-019), and at most one more
    from a public counter held as 32-bit words on the same path, so the same count applies at every
    width (FR-022); a candidate whose ARX-box needs an addition and a dependent operation in one
    step spreads them over two phases. A stage is one colour phase and takes
    one clock cycle, so a round of P phases takes P cycles, where P is the number of phases in one
    round of the candidate's operation list at that size: a colour visited twice counts twice, and P
    = 2 is the case of a red-black schedule that visits each colour once. FR-020's formulas,
    FR-026's rate and cost per output bit and research R5's cost model use that one P, recorded per
    candidate and size in its log entry (data-model §3), and carry chains are
    counted per phase. Within a stage every update reads the values at the
    phase's start, as the interpreters do, which the phase rule, checked by the
    loader at every supported size, makes equal to list order (FR-019). A
    subtraction counts as one chain, as an
    addition does; a public counter held as 32-bit words counts where it sits on a stage's
    path, and not when it is registered on a path of its own. The design document gives each
    stage's estimated delay against the 5 ns of 200 MHz on the XC7A100T −1, with its source, and
    the LUT levels on its longest path, as estimates that feature 008's post-route timing
    checks; they are reported, not eligibility rules.
  - **Registers and read-back.** All state is registered, and there is no read-back path for
    key or state. This covers the RTL's ports and, on the board, the configuration readback and
    any debug core (an integrated logic analyser, JTAG user registers): feature 008 builds the
    production bitstream with readback security set and no debug core, and records it. The design
    document gives the port list, with every port's direction, width, value after configuration and
    value after reset, the command interface's ports, `selftest_fail` and the error-code output
    among them, and the rule of no read-back path is checked against it (FUT-HW-5).
    Constructed states (FR-054) run on the RTL in simulation only, loaded by the testbench
    (VHDL-2008 external names or `force`), so the synthesizable RTL has no load or read-back
    port; on the board, FUT-HW-4's equivalence uses the vectors a key reaches, through the
    normal interface. In the model, `Generator` exposes no state, key or counter through any
    public method or attribute, and `pi`, `pi_inv`, `pi_trace` and `ResearchDuplex`, named
    research-only, are the only places that show state (FR-033); Python cannot make memory
    unreadable, so this rule concerns the interface.

    reset sequence: serial key loading along the registered shift chain below, the three latencies
    stated below, an output-valid signal, and the zeroization of key and state. The key-loading
    interface is generic in c: a c-bit key, with the c/2-bit nonce given at each instantiate on the
    absorption interface (research R14), 256 and 128 bits at every claimable
    size, both whole 32-bit words, since a keyed variant's c is a multiple of
    64; keyless variants have none, and FUT-GRID-2's key loading "for every
    supported size" is met through the keyed variant each size has (FR-018). The core is
    vendor-neutral and takes its clock as an input, on the device's clock network; clock generation
    belongs to the board wrapper: a wrapper of feature 008, outside the core and the only file with
    vendor primitives, derives the clock from the 100 MHz oscillator through an MMCM, and the
    reports give the wrapper's and the core's timing apart (FUT-HW-1, FUT-HW-2). The wrapper's
    duties toward the core's reset are stated here and carried to feature 008, as the clock split
    is: it holds the core's reset while the MMCM reports no lock and for a stated number of cycles
    after lock, releases it synchronously to the derived clock through a two-stage
    synchronizer, synchronizes every asynchronous reset request (from the board,
    a button or the host) the same way, and asserts reset again whenever
    lock is lost, so that the core sees only a synchronous reset. Reset is
    synchronous and reaches the nodes through a registered
    fan-out tree whose latency is part of the reset sequence, and the round and phase counters
    are replicated, one copy at each food node or node, all started by that reset and running
    in lockstep, so that no counter value crosses the grid. The latency is given as three figures
    in cycles, fixed by public values: from reset released to the self-test done and the state
    zeroized (ready for a key), the self-test's key and nonce words fed through the chains included
    (FR-059); key loading, from the last key word accepted to the key held, for a finished load, a
    call of its own whose cycle count joins FR-020's list; and instantiate, from the last nonce or
    personalization word accepted to instantiate done, its forget call complete and the core
    `instantiated` and ready for a command, the trees' latency and the nonce chain's length included
    (FR-018). A generate request's own figure runs from its acceptance on the command interface
    ("Flow control") to its first output valid, and the figure of a zeroize from `loading`
    ("Call sequence") is stated with the other triggers' ("Zeroization").
    The key path is specified with them: a key register at each node that holds key bits, loaded
    once per key along a registered shift chain through those nodes and copied into its lanes in one
    cycle at every instantiate. The host gives the nonce on the absorption interface at each
    instantiate, in the model's word order; it reaches its lanes through the absorption tree where
    they are rate lanes, and otherwise along a nonce chain of its own beside the key's, through the
    nodes that hold nonce bits in lanes outside the rate, so the key chain shifts only at key
    loading. Each rate lane that holds nonce bits has a nonce register at its node, filled through
    the absorption tree from the nonce words' transfer on the absorption interface; at the copy the
    selector writes that register into the lane, never as an XOR, so that every lane is set in that
    one cycle and the initial state is the one FR-013 and research R10 place. Each chain
    passes, registered, through the nodes between those that hold its bits, so
    no path of neighbours is required, and its length counts those registers. At each instantiate a
    selector at every lane sets the lane, in the same cycle as the copy, from one of
    four sources: a key register, the nonce chain, the nonce register of a
    rate lane that holds nonce bits, or its stated constant (FR-013), and
    the selectors and the nonce registers are parts of FR-026's estimate.
    The key chain's length enters the key-loading figure, the nonce chain's
    length, the nonce registers' transfer and the copy the instantiate figure,
    the plan's Complexity Tracking lists both chains as local data paths of
    public length, and zeroization clears both chains, the nonce registers and every copy.
  - **Call sequence.** The design document gives the call sequence as a state table, with the calls
    each state accepts, each call starting with its command word on the command interface ("Flow
    control"). `not-reset`, the state after FPGA configuration and before the first reset, accepts
    no call: every register of the core has a stated initial value of zero, given as initial values
    in the VHDL that configuration applies, the error-code register among them, where zero means no
    error and the codes start at 1, and output-valid stays low until a reset has run the self-test
    (FR-059); feature 008's board wrapper also holds reset after configuration, until the MMCM locks
    and for a stated number of cycles after lock ("Key loading and reset"). `resetting` runs from
    reset released to the self-test done and the state zeroized. `failed`, after a failed self-test,
    accepts no call until the next reset, with `selftest_fail`, an output of its own, latched. In
    `not-reset`, `resetting` and `failed` the core holds ready low on the command, key-loading and
    absorption interfaces and output-valid low, so no call is accepted and no error code is raised.
    `no-key`, after a reset with a passed self-test or after a zeroization, accepts a key load only,
    which enters `loading` at its first key word; `loading` runs from the first key word to the last
    and accepts the remaining words and zeroize only, the key being held only after
    the last word, so an instantiate is refused with its code and changes
    nothing, and a zeroize clears the chain and returns to `no-key`, where
    a new load starts afresh; `key-held` accepts instantiate and zeroize;
    `instantiated` accepts the calls the model's call sequence allows, and zeroize. Every other call
    is refused with its error code ("Zeroization") and changes nothing, among them a key load while
    a key is held (zeroize first, so no state from the old key survives into the new key's use) and
    an instantiate with no key. The refusals match the model's `CallSequenceError` rules where the
    model has the state, and FUT-HW-5's "key zeroization on reset" is tested from the ports: after a
    reset, an instantiate is refused as `no-key`. The tests cover a call in each state of the table.
  - **Flow control.** Each interface (command, key loading, absorption, output) uses valid and
    ready. The command interface takes one 32-bit word per call, with the call type (key load,
    instantiate, generate, reseed, zeroize) in stated bits and, for generate, the length in 32-bit
    words in the rest; a refused command is answered on the error-code output ("Zeroization"), and
    the length word of a personalization string or a reseed input stays on the absorption interface.
    The core never stalls in mid-call: output leaves through a buffer of one block (r bits), and the
    next call starts only after the consumer has taken the block, so a stall, which the consumer
    sets in public, holds only the buffer and delays the next call, and no cycle count depends on
    secret data. While a block waits in the buffer, the command interface accepts zeroize, which
    clears the buffer, the trees and the state, and no other call is accepted
    until the block is taken or a zeroize or reset clears it. Draining a
    block therefore never overlaps the next call of Π, and FR-026's rate
    counts the drain; a design that overlaps them needs a second buffer, which the design document
    may describe as a variant with its own cycle formula, size and clearing rule. The one-block
    buffer is the output register that zeroization clears, and it and the registers of both trees
    are cleared when a request ends, with or before its forget call, so the rest of a partial last
    block is discarded by clearing, not left in place; the clearing's cycles join FR-020's formulas,
    and feature 006 checks it in simulation (FR-058). The model holds no buffer. The start signal
    reaches the nodes and the replicated counters through a registered
    fan-out tree with one stated latency to every node, as reset does, so lockstep holds; the call
    type travels with it on that tree, as a field of ⌈log₂ t⌉ bits for t call types registered
    beside it at every node, and selects each food node's ROM stream where the call types draw on
    distinct streams (FR-023). The buffer's size and the tree's latency enter the cycle formulas of
    FR-020, which count every cycle of a call except those in which the core waits for
    the host, on every interface, and the plan's Complexity Tracking. The
    design document states the interface formats: 32-bit transfers on the command, key-loading and
    absorption interfaces; a personalization string or a reseed input preceded by
    one 32-bit word giving its length in bytes, refused above the design's maximum; unused bytes of
    a last partial word that must be zero, a word with a nonzero unused byte being an error path,
    with its error code ("Zeroization"), since it can arrive after earlier blocks of the same input
    have been absorbed; and the key and the nonce as whole 32-bit words, since a keyed variant's c
    is a multiple of 64 (research R14), in a stated word and byte order, matching the model's. A
    generate request gives its length in 32-bit words in its command word, so a length that is
    not a whole number of words cannot be expressed, as the model refuses an `nbits` that is not a
    positive multiple of 32, and a length of zero is refused.

    key-loading buffer (the key chain's registers) and the nonce chain beside it, the rate lanes'
    nonce registers, the held key and its copies, and the output register (the one-block
    buffer of "Flow control"), and returns the round, phase
    and limit counters to their reset values. Its triggers are reset, an explicit zeroize, a
    failed self-test, an error path and a call refused at a per-key limit (the call that would pass
    the limit; nothing happens when a counter merely reaches it), each with its latency in cycles
    and output-valid low throughout. Every trigger drives the reset's registered fan-out tree, so a
    zeroization reaches every register within the reset's latency, which each trigger's figure
    counts. A zeroization by an explicit zeroize, an error path or a per-key limit runs no self-test
    and goes to `no-key`; a failed self-test's zeroization goes to `failed` and starts nothing
    further; only reset runs the self-test ("Call sequence"). The core has a
    registered error-code output beside the error signal and valid with it,
    with one code for each refusal that changes nothing (a length, a call out of sequence, any other
    limit) and one for each zeroizing refusal (a per-key limit, an error path), listed in the design
    document, so the host can tell from the ports which refusals zeroized. An error path is a
    condition the RTL detects after the state has changed: the design document lists every such
    condition, each of which zeroizes, raises the error signal with a zeroizing code and needs a new
    key, among them a last partial word with a nonzero unused byte ("Flow control"), and lists every
    other check made part-way through an input with its class; a bad request that valid and ready
    refuse before anything changes is a refusal that changes nothing; reset and clock glitches stay
    with feature 008 (FR-008). Every path that leaves key-dependent state behind is specified and
    tested (FR-058): a refused request changes nothing, and the object or device stays usable; a
    failed self-test zeroizes and leaves it unusable, the device until the next reset, which runs
    the self-test again (FR-059), while in the model no object exists (FR-058); an exception after
    the state has changed zeroizes the whole state and ends the object, so no half-updated state
    survives; and a call refused at a per-key limit zeroizes and ends the object, since nothing more
    may be done with that key. After reset or zeroization, output-valid stays low and the output
    register holds zeros until an instantiate requested by the host completes after a passed
    self-test, and a generate request before then is refused with an error signal, as the model
    raises `CallSequenceError`. The self-test's blocks go only to its comparator, which takes each
    whole r-bit block as the output buffer receives it, with no consumer handshake, so they never
    reach the output port, and the zeroization after the self-test clears what
    it computed, both chains included (FR-059). In the model, zeroization
    overwrites the object's own `bytearray` copy of the key with zeros in place and drops it, and
    drops the state's integers, which Python cannot overwrite; as with constant time, this is a
    discipline that governs what the object keeps, not what Python's memory may still hold, and the
    caller's own copy of the key is outside it (model contract). That sentence says what the model
    does not promise, so it stands through an allow-list entry of the claim test (FR-069).

    objects in the model (FR-007), and the design document lists them with the other usage

  stated per size (for example 32 bits per cycle at every size, as the vector files list words,

**Reproducibility**

- **FR-053**: Every evidence record MUST be reproducible from a committed script with fixed
  inputs and the recorded command, with the model hash and tool versions recorded (Principle V).
  `requirements.txt` MUST be corrected and its versions pinned (checklist group F), the Hypothesis
  pins entering it once their wheels are installed and checked (research R18). These rules
  make that checkable:
  - **Reproducible, per kind.** Deterministic kinds (vectors, constants, dependency matrices,
    counts, digests) reproduce byte for byte; searches and solver runs reproduce their values
    and verdicts. A reproduction is run by the script's `--reproduce RECORD` mode, the re-check's,
    the generator checks' and the cross-checks' included, from a separate worktree of the
    original's `git_head` whose blobs its code is checked against, and writes a
    `reproduction` record of its own, naming that commit; a generator's reproduction
    compares each generated file in memory with the SHA-256 its `generator-run`
    record holds (data-model §11); it appends nothing to the candidate log,
    charges no candidate's budget row (a heavy one, run only on the owner's
    request, charges research R7's feature-wide row) and is not a re-run
    under research R7, so it can neither replace evidence nor add any, and a
    difference is a finding: the original stands, a difference in a field that enters a verdict
    blocks the candidate's eligibility, `blocked`, until it is resolved (research R7, R16), and a
    difference in a field that decides no verdict, such as a p-value, is reported and blocks
    nothing. It compares the fields data-model §1 lists per kind (`kind`,
    `model_id`, `variant_id`, each source file's entry in `code_sha256`, `inputs`, each
    deterministic output's `sha256` and `bytes`, `exit_status`, `assertions`, `status`,
    `classification` and the fields of its kind, and `design_sha256` only for the kinds whose code
    reads the design document) and reports every other field only, timings and raw solver logs among
    them; a different source hash makes it a new run, not a reproduction. A `p_values` output is
    marked not deterministic and compared by its values, within the tolerance data-model §1 defines;
    a value outside makes the outcome `differs`, a finding about the run's
    reproducibility that blocks no candidate, since a p-value decides nothing. The
    verdict is recomputed under the spec version, the research version and the protocol change-log
    entry the original cites, and a reproduction runs in the environment that made the original, as
    recorded, a `recheck` record's reproduction excepted, which runs anywhere, as the re-check does
    (research R1).
  - **Code by hash.** Every record holds the SHA-256 of every code file the run used (FR-061).
    The model hash is that of the candidate's operation list before selection and that of
    `model_v3.py` after it. Code is committed before every evidence run; `git_dirty` may be true for
    data and reports only, never for a file a record cites (data-model §1), and
    the record lists the dirty paths: a run that writes evidence exits 2
    unless every source file in its `code_sha256` matches HEAD, committed and unmodified, which
    constitution 1.3.0's standing authorization to commit makes routine, and a binary is rebuilt
    from committed source, its hash reported only. Each record's code is then found at its
    `git_head`, and a test checks each `code_sha256` entry against the blob at that commit. A
    reproduction runs the original's code at its `git_head`, and the guard applies to that commit.
  - **Canonical order.** Every list in a record or vector file is sorted by a stated key, so
    that nothing depends on set iteration, hash randomization, thread completion or worker
    count. Records hold `PYTHONHASHSEED` if it is set, and the worker and thread counts; a test runs
    a stated test operation list with two worker counts and compares the outputs. That test is a
    heavy run (research R7): it takes the lock, projects first, charges no candidate, only research
    R7's feature-wide row, and is not evidence.
  - **Hashes and line endings.** Each recorded hash says beside it whether it is over the raw
    bytes or over LF-normalized content (`raw` or `lf-normalized`). Every new file whose hash
    is recorded is hashed over its raw bytes and MUST have a pinned line ending in
    `.gitattributes` (LF, or `binary`), so that its raw and normalized content agree on every
    checkout: `reports/004-crypto-architecture-v3/**`, `research/004/**` and `protocol/*.json` are
    pinned to LF, and `*.gz` to `binary`. Files committed under
    `reports/004-crypto-architecture-v3/` are limited to `.json`, `.jsonl`, `.md`, `.csv`, `.txt`
    and `.gz`, and `protocol/evidence.py` refuses any other suffix there, as research R16 and the
    data-model introduction state; `research/004/**` holds the plan's Python code beside files of
    those suffixes, and `protocol/*.json` JSON only, each pinned to LF as above. Every `.gz` file
    this feature writes is written by `gzip.compress(data, compresslevel=9, mtime=0)`, and its
    record also holds the SHA-256 of the uncompressed content, which a reproduction compares for
    every `.gz` file but a `p_values` file, whose values it compares instead (data-model §1). A test
    checks the header of every committed `.gz` file under this feature's paths
    (`reports/004-crypto-architecture-v3/`, `research/004/`, `vectors/phyco-v3/` and
    `baseline/phyco-v2/`); feature 003's `.gz` files, listed with their hashes in its `SHA256SUMS`,
    are outside its scope and stay unchanged. An existing `text=auto` file that a new record hashes,
    such as `security.md` or `README.md`, is hashed over its LF-normalized content, and no existing
    pin changes. The CRLF-pinned freeze records of features 002 and 003 keep their recorded hashes
    over CRLF bytes. A test lists every path whose hash a committed record, manifest or freeze
    record holds and checks with `git check-attr text eol` that each has an explicit rule or
    `binary`, or that its record marks the hash `lf-normalized`; it replaces the one-off check of
    2026-10-31 and runs from plan phase 1 on, so that a path missed later fails it as soon as the
    first record that cites it is committed.
  - **Encoding.** Every new text file is UTF-8 without a byte-order mark, with LF line endings.
    Scripts write with `encoding="utf-8"` and `newline="\n"`, the line-ending test also looks
    for a byte-order mark, and no hashed file is written by Windows PowerShell 5.1, whose
    `Out-File -Encoding utf8` adds one.

### Pre-registered Design Protocol

Fixed at spec approval; afterwards it may only become stricter (FR-044). Each rule states what
stricter means for it, and every change is recorded in the protocol change log with its reason
and the owner's approval (FR-044).

**Development inputs.** Keys, nonces, personalization strings and test states are development
inputs, derived from public strings by a rule the plan fixes before their first use, as feature

of constructed inputs is allowed, each fixed by a public rule before its first use (research R3;

alternating, counting; the single-bit keys and nonces e_i for every i); (2) the edge states of

flips and the word rotations of development inputs, for every j, bit and k the rule names; (4)
feature 002's `dev` seeds for FUT-SEC-8, the one external development set; and (5) synthetic counts
for the bias test's validation only, drawn by a public rule from seeds hashed from public strings
(research R3). No value or index is picked after any output is seen. A run that is not evidence,
such as the 60 s projection before a heavy run or any timing probe, writes a record of kind
`projection` with its inputs (the first inputs of the run it projects); its outputs other than
timing are discarded unread, the record says so, and its time counts against the budget row of the
run it projects. *Stricter means*: the derivation does not change.

**Round rule.** For every claimable size,

```text
R = max(2 · R_dist, R_bound + Δ, 2 · max(R_f, R_b))
```

Every term counts rounds, a round being one pass over every colour phase (FR-029), and every term
comes from the production width at that size; a reduced-variant result is evidence about the
design, never a value of the rule (FR-032).

- R_f, R_b: from the exact dependency matrices (FR-029).
- R_bound: the smallest number of rounds after which the proven bound on the best differential
  trail probability and the proven bound on the best linear trail's squared correlation are both
  below 2⁻¹²⁸, from bounds (FR-038) that hold at the production width and that size. If it is
  not established, the size cannot be frozen. Before selection, R_bound at each claimable size comes
  from a bound computed at that size; a proof that carries a bound to a larger size is written for
  the selected candidate only and serves as a check beside the computed bounds (FR-038).
- R_dist: the largest number of rounds of Π or Π⁻¹ for which this feature's evidence shows a
  distinguisher costing less than 2¹²⁸ evaluations of Π or Π⁻¹ (FR-004): incomplete diffusion,
  a bias that meets the bias rule below, a probabilistic invariant (FR-037), a trail of Π found with
  probability or squared correlation above 2⁻¹²⁸ (a characteristic, its difference or mask after
  every round with its exact weight, exhibited by a validated tool that searches Π at the production
  width and that size and held in a `trail` record, while a bound of any kind enters R only
  through R_bound; FR-038), or a degree-based distinguisher (zero-sum, cube,
  or an inside-out zero-sum over r₁ rounds of Π and r₂ of Π⁻¹ from a middle state) that the
  candidate's `degree` records admit by the stated rule's counts, w − 1 per addition, which are kept
  where they are not upper bounds, computed per candidate and claimable size before
  this rule is applied (FR-034; research R13). Feature 005's campaign may
  raise R_dist; a higher R is then a new model ID (FR-028).
- Δ = 2. It may only be raised.

At each size the kinds of evidence run in this order: trail bounds (R_bound), then the structural
checks (R_f, R_b, symmetries, invariants and the `degree` records), then the frequency test, then
the avalanche test (plan phase 5; research R4). The bias tests start at the R the earlier kinds
give, max(2 · R_dist, R_bound + Δ, 2 · max(R_f, R_b)), and R rises as research R4 says. Any later
rise, from either bias test, a re-run after a reproduction's finding or a stricter change, makes
every record that depends on R again at the new variant, each a new measurement of its kind, charged
to its budget row where research R7 budgets the kind, in research R7's order: first the constant
table for the new R with its checks (research R15); then the inverse and invariant checks; then the
`cost` records and the reference outputs (research R5, R11); then every bias test already run at
that size, extended to the new R and charged to the 12 h row. The propagation through instantiate
and its margin over R_f are computed again (FR-029). A check made again that fails or fires raises R
again or makes the candidate `ineligible`, as its kind says, and the sequence repeats until R no
longer rises; if a row cannot hold a measurement it needs, that evidence is inconclusive and the
candidate `ineligible`. Where the call types draw on distinct constant streams, a rise changes the
constants of every call type after the first in research R15's reading order from round 1, so their
results are measured again from round 1, and every record names the constant table it read by its
SHA-256 (FR-023).

*Stricter means*: Δ only rises, and sources of R_dist are only added.

**Bias rule.** At each size and round count, a state bit's frequency of ones counts as a
distinguisher if its two-sided binomial p-value is at most 0.005/S, where S is the number of bits
tested at that size and round count on one call type's permutation, S = 4b
(research R4); where the call types draw on distinct constant streams (FR-023),
each call type's permutation is tested at its own threshold, and a firing in
any of them raises R. The number of development inputs per measurement is
fixed in the plan before any measurement. By the Bonferroni inequality, the rule
fires on uniform states with probability at most 0.005 for one permutation.
Before its first use at each size, it is applied once to the same number of
uniform states, derived from public strings by SHA-256 as the development
inputs are; if it fires there, the bias evidence of that size is inconclusive (security.md
§11.6). A firing is reported as a distinguisher at its round count that raises R,
with the family-wise rate of chance firings for the call types tested stated
beside it (FR-028), and it is never measured again to remove it. At each size
it runs after the trail bounds and the structural checks, from the R they
give, and a later rise of R extends it to the new R (round rule). *Stricter means*: only a change
that can add firings and never remove one. Every
firing recorded under the rule then in force stands, and a larger N, N_a or F, or a new set, is
tested under its new S or S_A in addition, so R can only rise.

**Nonlinearity choice rule (D4).** Each family is represented by its best candidate on reduced
widths. The family chosen is the one with the larger proven weight per round (−log₂ of the
weaker of the differential and linear bounds, per round, over the rounds computed) per unit of
estimated hardware cost (LUTs per node and round, from an operation-count model the plan fixes
before any candidate is scored). The rotation amounts each candidate is scored with are ranked by
the score's own measure, the −log₂ of the weaker of the two bounds at the search width, at one
number of rounds for every candidate, fixed in the protocol change log before the first rotation
search (FR-024). Only bound records of a tool that has passed its cross-check on
that candidate count for the choice, the cross-checks running first in the
candidate's trail row, on the first amounts of its recorded space (FR-039;
research R7, R12). Ties go to the smaller R from the round rule, then to fewer
serial carry chains per round, then to the owner's recorded choice. That choice is made only after
the tie record (`d4/tie.json`) exists, never changes, and is recorded beside it, in
`d4/tie-choice.json`, which `d4_choice.py --choose` writes against the SHA-256 of the record the
owner saw, and in its own row of the Review & Approval table ("D4 tie"), with the date and both
records' SHA-256; the tie record stays as the script wrote it. The production-width analysis then
runs on the chosen family. The other family is analysed at the production width only if the chosen
family ends with no eligible candidate, once every candidate of the chosen family has its final
decision (Eligibility and selection), in research R7's order, its rows reserved meanwhile; if the
chosen family has an eligible candidate, each candidate of the other family not analysed there gets
`ineligible`, with the reason "not analysed at the production width: D4 chose the other family",
which the final report states. Where D4 is not computable both families are analysed, and an exact
tie sends both representatives on (research R6). *Stricter means*: no change.

**Tool validation.** A tool's result, positive or negative, counts only after its validation
record exists (FR-039). *Stricter means*: known results and re-validation triggers are only
added.

**Eligibility and selection.** A candidate is eligible for the freeze only if it passes every

computed at that size (FR-038), and meets FR-018 to FR-031, each item decided
for every candidate before selection by `round_rule.py` from its operation
list, log entries and records (data-model §9): FR-018's folding rule, longest link, food nodes with
their reason and tree figures from its operation list and `parameters` entry; FR-020's cycles per

after scaling from its log entries; FR-025's stages, chains per phase and key path from its
operation list's declarations, checked by code; FR-027's semantics from its operation list's
declarations; and FR-030's mapping from its log entry. FR-025's interface formats, call sequence and
zeroization rules are properties of the mode, the same for every candidate, decided once in the mode
chapter, and the self-test's expected output (FR-018) is generated from the selected candidate's
vectors and cannot fail. A missing item fails eligibility. The design document restates the items
for the selected candidate, and the owner design review checks that the two agree. "Passes"
means that every measurement is conclusive (its controls did not fire, no tool defect is left
unresolved and no reproduction that differs from its original on a field that enters a verdict is
unresolved; FR-053), whatever R_dist it gives, and that R_dist, R_f, R_b and
R_bound are established at every claimable size; inconclusive bias evidence at a size makes the
candidate ineligible. Of the eligible candidates of the chosen family, or of the other family if the
chosen one has none, or, where the D4 record's outcome is `not computable`, of both
families, the one with the smallest R at 4×4 is frozen; a tie goes to the owner's
recorded choice, made only after the selection record (`selection.json`, or a
later `selection-<k>.json`) exists, never changed, and recorded beside it, in
`freeze/selection-tie-choice.json` (`freeze/selection-<k>-tie-choice.json`), which `round_rule.py
--choose` writes against the SHA-256 of the record the owner saw, and in its own row of the Review &
Approval table ("Selection tie", or "Selection tie <k>"), with the date and both records' SHA-256; a
choice binds only the record whose hash it names, so a new tie needs a new choice, and every
selection record stays as the script wrote it. Any change before the freeze that touches any
candidate's eligibility or R at 4×4 after a selection record exists makes `round_rule.py --select`
run again over the whole set, writing `selection-<k>.json`, k = 2, 3, …, which cites the previous
record; earlier records stay, and a decline's re-selection is one such record. If another candidate
is selected, the security-target review and every later review are held again for it, each in a row
of its own; if the same one is, the reviews held stand for what did not change (FR-043). Every
candidate stays in the log (FR-041). Candidates are entered before any is scored
(the entered set of research R6), and a further candidate is entered later only in place of one that
became `rejected` or `ineligible`, never because another's R is high: one is entered for every such
candidate while its family has fewer than three evaluated candidates and the budget allows, proposed

**Stop rule.** At most three candidates of each family (D4) are evaluated. A candidate is a
distinct design of Π with its own entry in the candidate log (FR-041), eligible or not, and it
counts as evaluated from its first entry, `proposed`, which comes before any tool runs on it; a
fourth `proposed` entry of a family is refused. A later candidate is entered
only in place of one `rejected` or `ineligible`, one for each such candidate
while its family has fewer than three evaluated candidates and the budget
allows, or once after a decline at the owner design review where the rules can
select it, never because another's R is high, and none after the set closes (Eligibility and
selection). The budget allows a replacement while each of its own rows of research R7, at its
current value after any lowering, holds more than zero hours; "the budget spent", the reason that
closes a `replacement` entry otherwise, means that a lowered budget left a row of the
further candidate at zero, which the log writer accepts only when R7's rows
and the protocol change log's entry that lowered the budget show it, exit 2
otherwise (data-model §3). A change makes a new candidate if it touches the
operation list's structure: the nonlinear component, the round structure
(its operations, their forms or order), the colour schedule, or the rule that generates the
constants or the rotation amounts. It is a variant or a fix if it changes only values the
candidate's logged rules derive (w, n, R, c), or corrects a value to what those rules give; so a
reduced variant, a round count, or a fix to the model that leaves the design unchanged is not a new
candidate. When in doubt, it is a new candidate. The analysis budget per candidate, such as the
solver time for trail bounds, is fixed in the plan before the first candidate is scored. The feature
recommends stopping (security.md §21, option C) if none of the evaluated candidates is
eligible, or if no published theorem's preconditions can be met by the mode,
or shown met, as when no theorem of research R10's list that covers a call
has its paper on disk and read (Edge Cases). No candidate is eligible, for
example, when an attack below the target reaches the full rounds of every candidate, or when R_bound
is not established at the production width for any of them (strategy note §10; D10). The budget may
only be lowered (FR-044): a budget lowered below the hours already used leaves zero hours, and a
new measurement under a stricter rule counts against the same budget row. A stop is a valid,
reportable outcome, reported with what was reached. After a stop, a later feature may evaluate
further candidates only under its own pre-registered protocol and new model IDs, and it counts
this feature's candidates, results and stop in its selection bias. *Stricter means*: budgets
and the candidate limit only fall.
